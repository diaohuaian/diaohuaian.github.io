01 / The current preprint

Abstract

We prove that the full Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for static isotropic elasticity uniquely determines both smooth Lamé parameters in a bounded smooth domain of ℝ³ under strong convexity. The proof uses an exact four-component Laplace-type reduction, while its decisive identities are obtained from physical elastic solutions.

The characteristic quotient of the reduced system has rank three, whereas the leading physical adjoint lift supplies only two directions. We construct the missing polarization from the first nonvanishing correction of a reduced amplitude in the kernel of that lift. Paired with fixed primal and adjoint quotient frames, the resulting complex geometrical optics solutions yield a complete family of holomorphically weighted 3 × 3 matrix moments through the physical Betti identity. Cauchy–Plemelj factorization converts these moments into an invertible relative transport normalized in the common exterior. Holomorphic dependence on the projective null direction and transport rigidity then determine both Lamé parameters.

02 / The main theorem

Main Result

Global uniqueness stated in the manuscript

The principal result stated in the current preprint is that equality of the full Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps for two admissible pairs forces both Lamé parameters to agree in the domain:

Λλ1,μ1=Λλ2,μ2⟹λ1=λ2,μ1=μ2.

The formulation allows the boundary of the domain to have more than one connected component and imposes no smallness, symmetry, separability, or analyticity assumption on the coefficient pair. Full hypotheses, proof statements and technical details are in the manuscript.

03 / Proof structure

Mathematical Approach

The proof in the joint manuscript is organized around a four-component Laplace-type reduction of the Lamé system and a rank-three characteristic quotient. A central difficulty is that the leading physical adjoint lift supplies only two independent directions. The missing third polarization is produced from the first nonvanishing correction of a reduced amplitude lying in the kernel of the leading lift.

Exact physical elastic solutions, paired with fixed primal and adjoint quotient frames, then provide holomorphically weighted 3 × 3 matrix moment identities through the physical Betti identity. A Cauchy–Plemelj construction converts those moments into an invertible relative transport normalized in a common exterior region. Projective holomorphicity and rigidity of the quotient transport supply the final identification of the material parameters.

05 / Research record

Research History and Independent Development

According to retained file records, an early research draft prepared by Umidjon Durdiev was saved on October 3, 2026 (UTC; October 4, China Standard Time), before the announced public release of the related OpenAI mathematical research collection on October 6, 2026.

This preliminary manuscript formulated a global uniqueness statement for the three-dimensional unit ball and developed key elements of the approach, including a four-component Laplace-type reduction, a rank-three physical quotient, the construction of an additional physical adjoint polarization, matrix moment identities, Cauchy–Plemelj relative transport, and rigidity of the Lamé quotient connection.

The research subsequently developed into the jointly authored paper Unique Determination of the Lamé Parameters in Three Dimensions, by Huaian Diao and Umidjon Durdiev. The collaborative mathematical development substantially strengthened and extended the original framework. The final manuscript establishes its main theorem for general bounded smooth domains and presents a substantially expanded proof, including refined CGO constructions and Sobolev remainder estimates, the realization of a complete physical adjoint frame, rigorous derivation of the full matrix moment identities, the construction of an exterior-normalized relative transport, and boundary determination to arbitrary order.

The final mathematical arguments, their verification, and the presentation of the results are the responsibility of both authors. The present paper represents their joint mathematical research.

The historical materials document the early private development of the research and the subsequent evolution of the manuscript. They do not establish an earlier date of public dissemination or constitute an independent determination of comparative scientific priority.
